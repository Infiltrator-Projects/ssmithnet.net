About · Online since 1993

A place of my own.

I'm Shannon Smith, in Mooroopna, Victoria. This is my personal website, not the front page of a company or a single software product. It began with a Mercedes-Benz 450SEL 6.9 in 1993. The interests grew from there, and the address remained a place to bring them together.

The original site

From one Mercedes
to a lifetime of projects.

An ongoing personal record, rather than a succession of unrelated websites.

The history below is about what the site has been used for, not a claim that every project dates back to 1993. I keep the vehicle story, software source and other machines under the same name because the connections between them matter.

1993

The 450SEL 6.9 and the Infiltrator website.

The original subject was my Mercedes-Benz W116 450SEL 6.9. Its engineering, specification and character gave the site something worth documenting. That beginning matters: the web page was personal first, and it still is. The Garage retains that vehicle alongside the others I have owned.

Beta-testing years

Windows, Solaris and games.

I was involved in voluntary beta testing of Microsoft Windows ME, Solaris and EA game projects. The Windows ME work included a Beta Tester of the Month award. Testing meant spending time with unfinished software and reporting what actually happened—not merely judging its intended feature list.

The software workshop

From applications to their foundations.

The software work expanded to include System Monitor, Defragmenter, Calendar, Calculator, vehicle diagnostics and InfiltratorFS. More recently the structure has grown into separately owned operating-system components, shared libraries, filesystem engines and specialist modules. These are different layers of work, not dozens of names for the same application.

Today

One website, several kinds of evidence.

Some pages are about source code; others are about cars, recorded factory details or physical audio equipment. The current Mercedes E 250 CDI links the Garage directly to MBLink development, while the B77 and dbx 224 are reminders that hardware can be interesting in its own right. The purpose is to keep the material accessible in its proper context.

Away from the code

Machines, music
and other interests.

The website is wider than the software projects.

Open-reel tape.

My ReVox B77 Mk II and dbx 224 combine mechanical transport, analogue recording and signal processing. The sound matters, but so does the physical machinery and understanding how it works. Their own equipment story belongs in the Workbench.

Films, games and cars.

Music, movies and games sit alongside the software work. Original games such as Egypt and Backyard Racer also connect creative interests back to programming. The vehicle history has its own Garage.

Mechanical timekeeping

My reverse-panda Daytona.

The Cosmograph Daytona 126500LN in Oystersteel has a black dial with contrasting pale chronograph registers and a black tachymeter bezel. The reverse-panda layout is the version I wanted to show here.

The attraction is familiar: mechanical precision, carefully finished metal and an instrument whose design reflects its purpose. It's another form of engineering that I enjoy living with, rather than just looking at on a screen.

Finding your way

Why the site has
separate sections.

The sections do different jobs, even when the subjects overlap.

Workbench

The long-form engineering explanations: why a design exists, where ownership lies, what a component currently does and what has not yet been qualified. This is where a filesystem or diagnostic protocol deserves more than a two-line project blurb.

Projects

The full public GitHub directory. Repositories are grouped by what they own, including calendar, clock and filesystem modules. It is a source catalogue, not a claim that every Git is a finished application.

Garage

The current Mercedes and a chronological record of thirteen earlier vehicles, with exact specifications where they are known and model-era dates clearly distinguished where individual manufacture dates are missing.

Software Centre

The distribution side: published Linux packages, repository trust and installation through APT. That is intentionally different from the complete source index, which also includes experiments, libraries and work still in development.
