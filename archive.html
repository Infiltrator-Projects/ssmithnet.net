About · Online since 1993

A place of my own.

I'm Shannon Smith, in Mooroopna, Victoria. This has been my personal corner of the internet since 1993—a place for the cars I've owned, software I've built, open-reel audio, watches and the machinery I enjoy understanding.

The original site

From one Mercedes
to a lifetime of projects.

An ongoing personal record, rather than a succession of unrelated websites.

The site began with one particular car and grew to record other interests over time. Its history is the story of that gradual expansion, not a claim that every project dates back to the beginning.

1993

The 450SEL 6.9 and the Infiltrator website.

The original subject was my Mercedes-Benz W116 450SEL 6.9. Its engineering, specification and character gave the site something worth documenting. That beginning matters: the web page was personal first, and it still is. The Garage retains that vehicle alongside the others I have owned.

Beta-testing years

Windows, Solaris and games.

I was involved in voluntary beta testing of Microsoft Windows ME, Solaris and EA game projects. The Windows ME work included a Beta Tester of the Month award. Testing meant spending time with unfinished software and reporting what actually happened—not merely judging its intended feature list.

The software workshop

From applications to their foundations.

The software work expanded to include System Monitor, Defragmenter, Calendar, Calculator, vehicle diagnostics and InfiltratorFS. More recently the structure has grown into separately owned operating-system components, shared libraries, filesystem engines and specialist modules. These are different layers of work, not dozens of names for the same application. The Workbench explains the architectural boundaries, while Projects lists the separate source repositories.

Today

One website, several kinds of evidence.

The Mercedes E 250 CDI is both a car I drive and an evidence vehicle for MBLink development. The ReVox B77 and dbx 224 are another kind of engineering interest: hands-on equipment that does not need to become a software project to deserve attention.

Professional background · ICT

Keeping systems working
for real people.

More than two decades of ICT management, infrastructure, support and technical delivery, alongside the software I develop independently.

My work has taken me through corporate contact centres, health, community services and education. I've led ICT teams, administered infrastructure and supported people who simply need their technology to work. It's a different kind of engineering from designing a filesystem, but the same attention to reliability, practical outcomes and understanding the system applies.

Away from the code

Machines, music
and other interests.

The website is wider than the software projects.

Open-reel tape.

My ReVox B77 Mk II and dbx 224 combine mechanical transport, analogue recording and signal processing. The sound matters, but so does the physical machinery and understanding how it works. Their own equipment story belongs in the Workbench.

Films, games and cars.

Music, films and games are part of the picture alongside the cars. Some ideas have also led to my own simulation projects, but enjoying those subjects came first. The Garage contains the record of the vehicles I have owned.

Mechanical timekeeping

My reverse-panda Daytona.

The Cosmograph Daytona 126500LN in Oystersteel has a black dial with contrasting pale chronograph registers and a black tachymeter bezel. The reverse-panda layout is the version I wanted to show here.

The attraction is familiar: mechanical precision, carefully finished metal and an instrument whose design reflects its purpose. It's another form of engineering that I enjoy living with, rather than just looking at on a screen.

The common thread

Different machines.
Same curiosity.

They don't have to be the same kind of project to belong here.

A filesystem raises questions about what can survive a failure. Vehicle diagnostics asks what a machine is actually reporting. An open-reel deck makes the signal path visible, and a mechanical watch is precision engineering small enough to wear. The interesting part is understanding how each behaves, not just knowing its specifications.

Some interests become code; others remain cars, music or physical equipment. I have kept them together on this site because one pursuit need not replace another. The original 450SEL 6.9 story still belongs here alongside the software and everything that followed.
